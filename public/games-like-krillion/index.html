If you enjoy finding the answer nobody else thinks of, take a flight with RARE. Answer five trivia prompts and watch a hot-air balloon climb through the clouds. Less obvious correct answers give you more lift.

Let’s take flight ↗

RARE is an independent game inspired by rare-answer trivia. Visit Krillion to play the original.

How high can you go?

Each flight has five questions, with 30 seconds to enter one answer per question. Topics include geography, science, history, everyday knowledge and movies. Every correct answer adds altitude according to a fixed editorial rarity tier.

Common+100 m
Uncommon+250 m
Rare+500 m
Exceptional+1,000 m

A wrong answer, skip or timeout adds zero. Your five stages combine into a final altitude of up to 5,000 meters. One server-scored point represents one game meter.

A daily flight and room to practice.

A fresh daily flight opens at midnight UTC. Each browser gets one attempt per date, with an archive available from November 12, 2026. Practice creates fresh five-question sets from the current English question bank.

If you arrived searching for Krillion practice or unlimited play, RARE offers its own flight game. Official Krillion modes and answers are available through Krillion’s website.

Unexpected answers catch the best updrafts.

Choose a specific correct answer that fits the question. Accepted aliases handle capitalization, punctuation, spacing and common name variants. Rarity tiers are editorial ratings, and correctness is checked on the server.

After every answer, your balloon catches an updraft and the altimeter tracks your gain. The following prompt stays hidden until you choose to continue. An active question’s timer keeps running if you close the page or open a dialog.

Your completed flight report includes all five stages, accepted answers and the daily leaderboard. Share a spoiler-free altitude card with friends.

Your own little flight log.

An anonymous cookie keeps your daily attempt. Browser local storage saves completed daily flight history. Clearing browser data removes that local identity. Sound is optional, and reduced-motion preferences disable the balloon and cloud animation.
